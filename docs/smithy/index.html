Safe learning demonstration

How an organisation could set up a service.

This page is a guided, made-up example. It shows three parts of setting up an information relationship. It does not send information anywhere and it is not connected to a real service.

Everything on this page is fictional.

Please do not enter real names, contact details, customer numbers or other personal information. In a real deployment, access would be restricted to authorised staff and governed by the organisation’s privacy, security and records-management processes.

1. Describe the service

Before sharing anything, an organisation should define the service and its boundaries: what it is for, what records it uses, and which people are responsible.

In the software this is called a “program”. Think of it as a documented service agreement, not a marketing program. Each program has its own rules and does not automatically give staff access to other programs.

2. Set up a connection

In a real service, this creates a separate connection between one person and one program. The connection uses an internal reference instead of putting a person’s general identity into every record.

Why make connections separate? A receipt from one service should not automatically reveal a person’s relationship with another service. Keeping connections separate limits unnecessary sharing.

3. Send a record

This example sends a made-up receipt. A real organisation should only send a record when it has a lawful purpose, a clearly communicated reason, and suitable safeguards.

What happens after a record is accepted? The service receives a dated receipt confirming its record was accepted. That receipt helps staff investigate a problem without silently changing history.
